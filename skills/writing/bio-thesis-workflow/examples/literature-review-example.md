# Example: Literature Review Paragraph

Heavy-ion mutagenesis differs from conventional chemical mutagenesis not only in the type of primary DNA damage it produces, but also in the repair routes that shape the final retained mutation spectrum. Studies across plant and non-plant systems consistently indicate that densely ionizing radiation is more likely to generate clustered lesions, short deletions, and structurally complex damage than alkylating mutagens. However, the degree to which these damage classes are fixed into heritable variants depends on species background, tissue state, repair context, and generation stage. In rice, existing work has established the breeding utility of heavy-ion treatment, but the evidence linking damage type, M1 chimerism, cross-generation fixation, and final functional-region distribution remains fragmented. This gap motivates a framework that connects mutagen source, repair logic, retained variant spectrum, and downstream candidate prioritization within the same evidence chain.
